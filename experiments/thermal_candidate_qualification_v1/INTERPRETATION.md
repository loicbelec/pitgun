## Original gameplay concerns

At the same eight-point era-I means and common normal recipe set, the engine/chassis-only 0440 profile is slower than the best sampled alternative by 171.665 s at Monza, 130.243 s at Silverstone and 134.449 s at Zandvoort. Allowing each profile the same legal SM gearing choices preserves gaps of 169.146/119.748/121.269 s. Concentrated preparation therefore does not dominate these samples. This is not a global search or a claim that a player can never win with any concentrated allocation.

At era-II entry, no discretionary cooling loses to the best sampled alternative by about 174–241 s across all seven regular contexts. At exit the installed Cooling1 remains present: extra cooling helps among sampled alternatives on four circuits, while no extra cooling leads on Silverstone, Monza and Zandvoort. Budget, engine/components and bonuses change together across entry/exit; this is not an isolated engine-architecture experiment. A universally positive cooling allocation is not an acceptance criterion.

One stop leads the sampled balanced strategies at era-I entry and era-II entry. At developed era-II means, one stop leads at Montreal/Spielberg, two at São Paulo/Silverstone/Monza/Zandvoort, and Mexico's one/two-stop plans are almost tied (.173 s). No sampled three-stop schedule beats both one/two-stop alternatives in this matrix. All service costs are still present and no asymmetric stop restriction was added. These are coarse recipes and selected timings, not global optima.

## Thermal behavior and retained advisories

The tires warm after initial/replacement preparation on every balanced SM context; they are not forced into a display band. Post-warm-up balanced SM surface means are about 86–87°C at era-I entry, 84–87°C at era-II entry and 114–121°C at developed era-II means. Entry cold exposure remains substantial (roughly 58–61% in era I); developed SM still has about 45–75% hot exposure. Those facts remain in the report. The successful screen's limited entry-grip guard does not identify real tire coefficients or make every compound/context thermally optimal. Surface and carcass are different states, and time means are not peak temperatures.

Two known limitations remain outside the declared qualification gates:

- **Early-aero/slider response (#469):** at developed/basic-aero balanced SM, the tested downforce .2 is faster than .5 on all seven circuits, and .8 is slower. This does not prove a global endpoint optimum, but it does not demonstrate an interior circuit-specific optimum either. Keep the audit visible; do not present the candidate as having every aerodynamic compromise finely calibrated. Point reallocations also move several axes, so these profile comparisons do not isolate every point coefficient.
- **Driver risk:** Attack remains faster in these few mode/seed controls despite additional recoverable lapses. They do not establish balanced long-run risk, accidents or VSC. Fixed traits and driver RNG are held comparable here; nine distinct actors and wider reserved seeds still need their own evaluation.

The zero-stop and high-wear/temperature extremes remain physical diagnostics. The provisional ceilings from the small ten-case thermal screen were not silently turned into universal safety limits for every aggressive/zero-stop preparation.
